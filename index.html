Summary

Thorfinn is a stone-built detached house on the north side of Thurso, most likely Edwardian (built after 1905, probably c.1906–1914) — the site is still open farmland on the 1904–05 Ordnance Survey revision, so "circa 1900" is slightly early. It is not listed. Five bedrooms (one used as an office), two bathrooms, slate roof, solid Caithness stone walls.

The documented ownership chain starts with Kate Macklon/Yanagimoto (Hattie's mother), in residence by June 2021. In April 2024 she transferred the house to her daughter Harriet Cleary as a family transfer (Inksters solicitors, Glasgow) — there was no open-market sale and no recorded price. Pre-2020 ownership, census occupants and the origin of the house name are all open gaps.

Occupants

Researched September 2026 — no free source yields occupants. What was established:

  • 1901: almost certainly irrelevant — the site was open farmland in 1904–05. (FamilySearch holds a free 1901 index, but it needs a free account.)
  • 1911 and 1921: exist only on ScotlandsPeople (pay-per-view); no free index exists anywhere — not on FamilySearch, Findmypast, MyHeritage, Ancestry or FreeCEN (whose Scottish coverage is effectively nil). Thurso registration district 041.
  • Valuation Rolls (annual, listing occupier and proprietor by address): indexed digital images for 1855–1940 on ScotlandsPeople, pay-per-view (about £0.50 per image). The relevant rolls are 1915, 1920 and 1925 (1905 to confirm absence) — but the search form does not accept house numbers, so a human must browse by surname + Caithness/Thurso.

Cheapest routes to the answer: a free ScotlandsPeople account (index searches are free; pay only per image viewed); the Highland Archive Centre, Wick, which holds Caithness valuation rolls for free in-person consultation; the NRS Finance Act 1910 field books ("the new Domesday" — owner, tenant/occupier, construction description and sketch map, exactly the build window); NLS digitised directories for Thurso c.1912–1925.
